Case Studies

Case studies across key technical areas

These are composite, anonymized scenarios representative of the type of engagements State & Lake is designed to support.

Details below are illustrative composites reflecting common engagement types. No confidential client information is disclosed.

Revenue Recognition: Principal vs. Agent on Pass-Through Vendor Costs

Risk

Recognizing revenue gross when net presentation was appropriate would have overstated reported revenue and distorted a metric investors watched closely ahead of a capital raise

Approach

Analyzed control indicators under ASC 606, including fulfillment responsibility, pricing discretion, and payment obligation to the third-party vendor irrespective of customer collection, and documented the conclusion against interpretive guidance from multiple national firms

Outcome

Gross presentation supported and accepted by auditors with no restatement of previously reported revenue

Consolidations: Thirty-Eight Partially Owned Entities Across Three Consolidation Models

Risk

A services operator held minority and equal-share interests in dozens of operating entities with inconsistent governance terms, creating risk that entities were consolidated or deconsolidated incorrectly across the portfolio

Approach

Built a repeatable seven-step decision framework applied contract by contract, evaluating the business scope exception, variable interest identification, primary beneficiary power and economics tests, the voting interest model, and joint venture criteria, and reconciled the differing thresholds between the VIE and joint venture literature

Outcome

Consolidation conclusions documented for every entity, with disclosure relief supported quantitatively; no audit adjustments

Transfers of Financial Assets: Loan Participation True-Sale Analysis

Risk

If a bank partnership arrangement failed true-sale treatment, the transaction would have been recharacterized as a secured borrowing, materially changing the balance sheet and revenue presentation for a rapidly scaling product line

Approach

Tested all four participating-interest characteristics under ASC 860, including proportionate cash flow allocation, priority equivalence, and pledge and exchange rights, then quantified whether the contractual servicing fee exceeded what would fairly compensate a substitute servicer

Outcome

Sale of a participating interest supported, with a documented framework that scaled as the program grew across additional markets

Goodwill Impairment: Reporting Unit Cleared by a Narrow Cushion in a Market Shock

Risk

A reporting unit that had cleared its prior annual test by a thin margin faced a sharp forecast reduction, putting a material impairment charge and the related disclosure directly in play

Approach

Built dual forecast scenarios with the CEO and CFO, engaged and evaluated a third-party valuation specialist, weighted the scenarios with documented rationale, and calculated the precise scenario weighting at which an impairment would be triggered

Outcome

No impairment recorded, with sensitivity disclosed transparently and the conclusion accepted without further inquiry

Regulated Operations: Market-Based Rates Failing the Regulatory Accounting Criteria

Risk

Two large infrastructure expansion projects had been accounted for under regulatory accounting from inception, but the underlying rates were market-based rather than cost-recovery, meaning regulatory treatment and capitalized financing costs were both potentially wrong

Approach

Applied the cost-recovery criterion to the substance rather than the form of the regulator's rate order, evaluated whether a separable-portion conclusion could be reached without unwinding regulatory treatment across the entire jurisdiction, and quantified the difference between regulated and non-regulated financing cost capitalization

Outcome

Regulatory accounting appropriately scoped out for the projects, with the quantified difference assessed as immaterial and passed on a documented basis

Business Combination: Related-Party Acquisition Requiring Control and Acquirer Analysis

Risk

The sellers were also among the largest shareholders and board members of the acquirer, and the acquirer had performed the target's accounting pre-close, raising questions about acquirer identification, acquisition date, and whether control had transferred earlier

Approach

Worked through the full acquisition method, evaluating the business definition screen, control transfer evidence, pre-existing service arrangements at fair value, intangible asset identification and completeness, and measurement period closure

Outcome

Purchase accounting finalized within the measurement period with no post-close adjustments and no auditor challenge to the related-party analysis

Restructuring: Workforce Reduction Spanning Three Separate Standards

Risk

A multi-state reduction in force combined voluntary programs, union and non-union involuntary separations, and pension curtailment, with each cost category carrying a different recognition trigger and timing

Approach

Segregated costs across the ongoing benefit arrangement, one-time termination benefit, and pension curtailment frameworks, resolved whether a substantive ongoing arrangement existed for a union population with no formal prior policy, and applied a forfeiture estimate to the accrual

Outcome

Restructuring charges recognized in the correct periods and categories, with disclosures reconciling the liability rollforward through completion

Debt: Refinancing Evaluated as Modification Versus Extinguishment

Risk

Proceeds from a new senior secured note issuance were used to redeem an existing series held partly by the same investors, and treating the transaction as a modification rather than an extinguishment would have deferred a substantial loss

Approach

Evaluated whether the redemption and new issuance were negotiated in contemplation of one another, tested the fact pattern against interpretive examples, measured new-investor participation, and documented an accounting policy election applied consistently

Outcome

Extinguishment accounting supported, with the applicable premium and unamortized financing cost write-off calculated and separately presented

Credit Facility: Creditor-by-Creditor Modification Analysis on an Amended Agreement

Risk

An amended and restated credit agreement changed the lender syndicate composition and individual commitments, and a portfolio-level conclusion would have misstated the deferred cost write-off

Approach

Performed the cash flow significance test at the individual creditor level for term loan participants and separately analyzed borrowing capacity changes for revolver participants, allocating unamortized costs pro rata for lenders whose capacity declined

Outcome

Write-off isolated to the exiting lender and one reduced-capacity lender, with remaining costs appropriately deferred over the new term

Derivatives: Cash Flow Hedge Documentation on an Interest Rate Swap

Risk

Failure to establish contemporaneous hedge documentation would have disqualified hedge accounting, forcing fair value changes through earnings and introducing volatility into reported results

Approach

Confirmed the instrument met the derivative definition, documented the risk management objective and hedged forecasted transactions, assessed counterparty default probability, and addressed the consequences if principal outstanding were to fall below the amortizing notional amount

Outcome

Hedge accounting supported from inception, with effectiveness testing and disclosure requirements established as a repeatable quarterly process

Equity Instruments: Warrant and Preferred Stock Classification in a Financing Round

Risk

Misclassifying warrants issued alongside preferred stock as liabilities rather than equity would have introduced recurring fair value remeasurement through earnings ahead of a planned public offering

Approach

Established that the warrants were freestanding by testing legal detachability and separate exercisability, then evaluated liability classification across the mandatorily redeemable, share-repurchase obligation, and variable-share categories before assessing derivative scope exceptions

Outcome

Equity classification supported for both instruments, documented to the standard the incoming auditors required for public company readiness

Divestiture: Seller Accounting for an Earn-Out With No Authoritative Guidance

Risk

A portion of the sale price was contingent over an extended earn-out period, and U.S. GAAP provides no authoritative seller-side guidance, leaving both the initial gain calculation and cash flow presentation exposed

Approach

Reconciled competing interpretations from two national firms, elected and documented a fair value policy applied consistently, engaged a valuation specialist to model the earn-out formula with sensitivity across growth and customer-attrition assumptions, and resolved cash flow classification across operating, investing, and financing activities

Outcome

Gain on sale recorded with a defensible fair value estimate, and a worst-case exposure quantified and confirmed immaterial

Uncorrected Errors: Restatement Assessment Under Quantitative and Qualitative Tests

Risk

Several out-of-period errors were identified mid-year across multiple prior periods, raising the question of whether previously issued financial statements required restatement

Approach

Quantified the errors under both the rollover and iron curtain approaches against a defined threshold, then evaluated all qualitative factors including whether the errors masked earnings trends, obscured a miss against analyst consensus, affected debt covenant compliance, or influenced management compensation

Outcome

No restatement required, with the aggregation conclusion documented and coordinated with internal audit on the related control implications

SOX 404: Control Deficiency Severity and Aggregation Assessment

Risk

Multiple control deviations were identified at year-end, several unremediated, creating exposure that individually minor issues could aggregate to a significant deficiency or material weakness in management's assessment

Approach

Documented each deviation with its root cause, identified specific compensating controls operating effectively during the period, assessed whether any financial statement risk could actually arise given system access architecture, and performed an aggregation analysis across processes and control owners

Outcome

All deviations concluded as control deficiencies not rising to significant deficiency or material weakness, with process changes implemented to prevent recurrence

Segment Reporting: Reportable Segments After Two Acquisitions in One Year

Risk

Two acquisitions in a single year changed how the chief operating decision maker reviewed results, and an incorrect segment conclusion would have affected goodwill allocation, impairment testing units, and non-GAAP disclosure

Approach

Reassessed operating segment identification against the CODM's actual reporting package, evaluated aggregation criteria and quantitative thresholds, and addressed the interaction between segment profit measures and non-GAAP presentation rules the SEC actively comments on

Outcome

Segment structure supported and disclosed consistently with internal reporting, with no SEC comment on the presentation

Going Concern: Liquidity Assessment Under Sudden Macroeconomic Stress

Risk

A sharp demand shock, a steep share price decline, and rising delinquency raised the question of whether substantial doubt existed about the ability to continue as a going concern, with disclosure consequences either way

Approach

Assessed liquidity sources against obligations coming due within the assessment period, evaluated covenant compliance headroom, incorporated a post-quarter financing facility, and weighed operational continuity factors alongside multiple forecast scenarios

Outcome

No substantial doubt concluded, with the analysis and monitoring plan reviewed and approved at the CAO and CFO level

IPO Readiness: Capitalizing Versus Expensing Offering Costs

Risk

A private company preparing to file had accumulated costs from underwriters, counsel, auditors, printers, and consultants, and misclassifying organizational costs as deferrable offering costs would have overstated assets and understated expense

Approach

Applied the specific-incremental-cost criterion invoice by invoice, allocated legal fees between offering and organizational activities with counsel's input, and established balance sheet, cash flow, and pro forma presentation for the deferred balance

Outcome

Deferred offering costs supported at the filing date with a cost-classification framework the company continued to apply through the registration process

Equity Method: Other-Than-Temporary Impairment Assessment on a Minority Investment

Risk

A significant minority investment carried at a material balance faced a macroeconomic shock, and the investor could not test the investee's underlying assets directly, limiting available evidence for the impairment conclusion

Approach

Evaluated each impairment indicator against available evidence, including performance relative to original investment expectations, peer and industry comparison, regulatory developments, and customer concentration, working directly with the investee's CFO to validate results reported on a lag

Outcome

No other-than-temporary impairment recorded, with the indicator-by-indicator analysis and the reporting lag both disclosed and accepted by auditors
